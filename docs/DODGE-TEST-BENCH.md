# 0.1.2 test exercises

Bat Splitting is directly in Skill Training. Its three stages use 6, 8 and 10 Gryphons and the same number of Batriders. Each bat initially has a unique Gryphon target. If that target dies from splash, the bat continues against a surviving Gryphon; if none survive, remaining bats are removed through death. Groups are 2/4, 3/3 or 4/2 at six units; 4/4 or 3/3/2 at eight; and 4/4/2, 5/5 or 3/3/4 at ten. The formation starts at the horizontally centered northern boundary of Arena 1. Each bat spawns 400 units beyond its assigned Gryphon's daytime sight radius, measured from that Gryphon's initial position. Two groups approach from opposite directions; three groups use evenly spaced directions. Native speeds remain unchanged, with no additional boundary. The enemy has arena vision throughout; group one receives Concoction orders at one second, group two at two seconds, and group three (if present) at three seconds after control transfers.

Black fog and unit sight are used instead of revealing the scene. During the command-locked countdown, per-unit shared vision exposes the neutral-owned Gryphons' native sight; this sharing is revoked when ownership transfers. Failure means any Gryphon died; neutral means all survived with splash damage; success means all survived without splash. Damage to a bat's assigned target is direct damage and does not count as splash. Results appear only after the last bat dies, with one simulation-step settling delay. Redo preserves geometry and target assignments. Next advances the stage; after ten units it starts a new series. Normal Mode freezes daytime at noon; Hard Mode freezes nighttime at midnight. Installed UnitBalance.slk gives Gryphons sight=1600 and nsight=900, so both modes spawn bats 2000 units away. Redo and Next preserve the selected difficulty. There is no ground battle or arbitrary round timer.

Dodging exposes 97 attack/spell cases and 34 combined dodge entries. The briefing directly offers Homing, Ranged, Artillery, Area and Devour. Targeted Impale, Storm Bolt and Frost Arrows are in Homing; coordinate Impale and Thunder Clap are in Area. Devour (Kodo) leads directly to the dodge-method question. Homing has three columns: Missiles, Ensnare and Orb Effects. Missiles lists Storm Bolt, Death Coil, Impale, Shadow Strike and Drunken Haze. Orb Effects lists Searing Arrows, Frost Arrows, Dark Arrows and Incinerate. Web and Cyclone are removed; Area retains two columns per race. Ranged opens a racial selection and the shared unit grid, disabling non-ranged units and Riflemen. The four artillery units also remain under Ranged for targeted attacks; the Artillery menu uses Attack Ground.

After choosing a danger, the question is "How do you want to dodge X?" Homing and Ranged use the grouped ability picker. Area, Artillery and Devour offer Movement or Ability. Movement uses the shared racial unit grids, with installed-game aliases normalized. Ability uses two vertical icon columns per category under gold Human, Orc, Undead, Night Elf, Neutral and Item headings. Shadowmeld uses its ability icon and name. Item users are ability-free Tinkers; ally-targeted staves retain a separate recipient and initially select their Tinker carrier. Shadowmeld-only female Night Elf entries were removed except Priestess of the Moon; their other dodge methods remain.

Homing and Ranged have three stages. Both units start at the native attack/cast range plus 200 in every stage. In stage one both can move; in stage two the pawn cannot walk. Both stages immediately order the enemy to attack or cast, allowing its native approach. In stage three the pawn cannot walk and the enemy first approaches to half range before targeting it. The runtime reads the caster's native ability/weapon range after research, with catalog data as fallback. Enemy arena vision avoids a sight-range dependency. Native spell-effect events confirm casts. Searing Arrows, Frost Arrows, Black Arrow and Incinerate enable their native autocast orb effect and issue an attack order. Native weapon attacks use the attacked event plus the damage-point delay and an order-continuity check, since attacked alone fires before missile launch. Homing verdicts follow native cancellation and impact evidence. A dodge command alone has no verdict: a pre-launch spell end/cancellation gives TOO EARLY, a confirmed hit gives TOO LATE, and a launched missile that finishes its conservative flight window without a hit gives WELL DONE. All displayed verdicts then resolve after one second. Failure clears the streak and resets the stage. Success increments the streak; one and two restore the setup, while three ends the stage. Ensnare requires a persistent net debuff, rather than a one-tick transient marker, to count as a hit. Damage is attributed to the selected caster. Outside Ensnare, fading invisibility/burrow methods succeed when they become hidden and prevent launch; a confirmed missile hit fails even if invisibility finishes afterwards. Wind Walk keeps the standard post-launch projectile-dodging goal. Treat the idea that Wind Walk alone can dodge launched projectiles as a working hypothesis for native testing. Projectile enumeration is unavailable, so launch and flight-window logic remain provisional until tested natively. In stage one the targeted pawn starts facing left; a confirmed launch displays GO without issuing an automatic movement order. Stage three submits its cast immediately upon arriving within 16 units of the half-range destination. Orb effects disable invisibility/burrow methods except Blademaster, plus Metamorphosis and Bear Form. Blademaster alone uses a single stage against orb effects, with three consecutive successful trials. Crow Form retains the armor explanation in its lower tooltip.

Ensnare's invisibility options are greyed out except Blademaster, and hovering still displays the requested revelation explanation. Divine Shield has its delayed-net explanation; Avatar, Anti-Magic Shell and Anti-Magic Potion have the magical-only explanation. Disabled hovering never invokes the option's action. Peon's lower tooltip is exactly Cast Point: 0. The Homing page keeps Dodging centered with - Homing to its right; only Missile and Orb Effects have column headings. The Wisp mine is 160 units from its initial position: installed 16x16Goldmine.tga has an eight-cell blocked center (128 units half-width), and the Wisp's 16-unit radius fits at the next 32-unit snap point.

Area and Artillery remain unscored tests. Artillery sits at the center of a circular arena whose diameter equals its native range. An inner circle marks the minimum-range dead zone; pawns are constrained to the annulus and hits are counted without early/late timing prompts. Devour uses a circular arena of radius 384 (twice the original half-size), with a continuously pursuing Kodo. Player units, including replacement handles, are constrained to visible arena bounds; buildings are not moved. Native restrictions remain active. Ordinary arena damage is recorded without resetting; a successful Devour restarts the setup. Completed area channels are allowed to resolve before another cast. An invalid target is not a successful dodge.

## Provisional categories

The original workbook's broad categories remain useful research labels; the exercise now uses the five menus above. The ranged inventory records native weapon types and target restrictions: missile, splash, bounce, line and instant weapons should not be assumed to behave identically. Point, immediate and unit-target orders are implementation details, not proof of dodge behavior.

Dodge categories are Movement, Invisibility, Teleportation, Invulnerability, Magic protection, Transformation, Replacement/removal, Loading/building and Dispel. The workbook compares categories, with a separate case inventory and test log for exceptions. Result cells deliberately start blank.

## Sources and reproduction

The initial inventory comes from the user's DodgeTraining.txt. Unit, ability and item identifiers and weapon types were checked against installed Warcraft III build 3.0.0.24268. `tools/read-warcraft-slk.py` reads extracted SYLK tables. `tools/prepare-dodge-catalog.py` consumes the tables and ability-function text files in ignored `work/`, validates identifiers, and writes `design/dodge-catalog.json` and `src/generated/dodge-catalog.lua`. Extract UnitData, UnitWeapons, UnitAbilities, AbilityData, ItemData, and race ability-function files from the installed CASC units directory before regenerating. The generated catalog is checked in so normal map builds do not require CASC extraction.

Category membership is a starting hypothesis; the bench must establish which effects can actually be avoided. The pasted LLM discussion is not treated as experimental evidence.

## Test controls and limitations

Pawns and opponents receive enlarged health pools to support repeated trials. Selected test abilities have mana costs removed and cooldowns refreshed; native target restrictions, damage resolution and missile behavior remain active. Opponents testing non-weapon spells have ordinary attacks disabled. Selected secondary weapons, such as Gargoyle air attacks and Barrage, exclude the other weapon. Helpers, research, items and nighttime are supplied where needed. Replacement/removal cases may require Redo when the original target disappears. Summoned and constructed units are tracked; a cleanup sweep removes unreported new player-owned handles while preserving the scene's existing units. Temporary items are explicitly removed. Units retain native health pools and receive all cataloged research. Builders and Obsidian Statue/Destroyer tests receive 10,000 gold and lumber. Player-owned replacements are also clamped; a death restarts the unscored test.

Tooltips show only the launch delay (labelled Cast point) and missile speed. Ordinary spell delay combines the caster animation cast point with the ability's pre-effect casting-time field; walking, turning and command processing add situational delay. Attack/orb timing is labelled as a base attack point because attack speed affects it. The native ability missile speed is used for orb arrows rather than the ordinary weapon projectile's speed.

Timing references: [first-hand ability timing tests](https://www.hiveworkshop.com/threads/does-casting-time-and-cast-point-stack.226163/) and [editor ability experiments](https://github.com/Cokemonkey11/wc3-ability-doc/blob/main/sources/screwthetrees-wc3-editor-ability-insight-document/2023-11-14.md). These identify arrows as attacks and attack-speed modifiers as affecting the animation. Exact manually cast versus autocast orb timings on the installed patch still require in-game measurements; the tooltip's base value is not a measured command-to-launch duration.

Success detection is based on cast/attack confirmation, damage/buff events and a conservative settling window, not direct enumeration of the engine's hidden projectile objects. In-game validation of launch cancellation, projectile lifetime, buffs, transformations, item triggers and zero walking speed is still required. The original spreadsheet remains a research artifact; no dodge outcome has been established merely by these code checks.

For each category pair, first verify that an unmodified attempt actually hits or applies its effect. Then try different dodge timings, repeat successful trials and note specific cases that differ. Record damage and debuffs separately. Test ground/air and friendly/enemy restrictions before interpreting a rejected cast. A category-level result may be partial or vary by case.

The three `tools/test-*.lua` files for training orders, Dodging lifecycle and Bat Splitting verify order routing, 102 staged setups/cleanup, category and grouped-picker navigation, hit reset, stage progression, preserved foundations, bat group sizes, unique targets, sight-distance placement and direct/splash attribution with mocked natives. Release packaging checks Lua syntax and archive integrity. These checks do not replace in-game tests.
